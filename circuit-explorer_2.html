Using Circuit Explorer

Laying out a circuit

Drag a part from the bin onto the board
Drag an end to rotate, stretch or join it
Drag the middle to slide the whole part
Ends within a few millimetres snap together
Symbols follow UK exam papers by default; switch Symbols to US for ANSI zigzags instead
Tick Colour‑code parts to tint them by type, or untick Circuit totals panel to clear the screen

Changing things

Click a part to open it in the inspector
Right‑click to remove a part
R flips the polarity of a selected cell
Drag a label to move it clear of the wiring
Double‑click a caption on a ready‑made circuit to retype it
Click a switch to open or close it

Sensors and sliders

Select a thermistor and drag its temperature slider — no heat source needed
Select an LDR and drag its light slider — 0% is full darkness
Drag the wiper arrow on variable resistors and potentiometers
Set Rmin and Rmax for swept components in the inspector
The voltmeter wires in like any other part — put it in parallel using connecting leads

Measuring without wiring anything in

Clamp meter — arm it, then hover over any lead or component to read the current there, without breaking the circuit to insert an ammeter
Potential probe — arm it, then hover anywhere on the circuit to read the potential at that point
Only one of the two is armed at a time; click the tile again to put it away
For a reading you can log or leave on the diagram, wire in a real ammeter or voltmeter instead

Capacitors and the data logger

A capacitor charges and discharges in real time — flip a switch and watch
The inspector shows Q, VC, E and the time constant τ
Choose meters for channel A and B, then press Record
Copy data puts the readings on the clipboard for a spreadsheet

Touchscreen

Press and hold a part in the bin, then tap the board to place it
Tap to select, drag to move
Long press on the board deletes, in place of right‑click
Double tap a caption to retype it

● Capacitors and the data logger

What a capacitor does here

A capacitor stores charge on its plates. The p.d. across it is set by how much charge it holds:
Q = CV     E = ½CV²
It is simulated in real time, so it fills and empties at the pace the circuit dictates. Through a resistance R the approach is exponential with time constant
τ = RC
After one time constant the capacitor has reached about 63% of the way; after five it is there for all practical purposes. The inspector reports τ for the resistance the capacitor can currently see.

Charging and discharging

1. Wire a capacitor in series with a resistor and a switch across a supply.
2. Close the switch — the p.d. across the capacitor climbs while the current decays.
3. Open the switch and close a second one across a discharge resistor to run it back down.
4. Empty capacitor in the inspector dumps the charge instantly so you can repeat the run.
5. Try the three ready‑made capacitor circuits for worked layouts.

Recording a curve

1. Put an ammeter in series with the capacitor, or a voltmeter in parallel with it — or both.
2. Choose them as channel A and channel B in the data logger.
3. Press Record, then operate the switch. The trace builds live against time.
4. Press again to pause; Clear starts a fresh run.
5. Copy data puts the whole run on the clipboard as columns you can paste into a spreadsheet and analyse — a log plot of V against t should be a straight line of gradient −1/RC.
6. Hover over the chart to read off a point.

Combinations

In parallel capacitances add: C = C1 + C2. In series the reciprocals add: 1/C = 1/C1 + 1/C2 — the opposite way round from resistors. The Capacitor combos preset sets both up side by side so you can check the totals for yourself.

● Saving and reopening work

Saving

1. Click Save circuit to turn the whole board into a block of text.
2. Copy it and keep it wherever suits — a document, an email, a lesson plan, a worksheet.
3. Everything is captured: parts, positions, connections, values, switch states, slider settings, captions, notes and every display option.

Reopening

1. Click Open circuit.
2. Paste a saved block in and confirm. The board is replaced by the saved circuit.
3. Ctrl+Enter opens it, Esc backs out.

Sharing

Codes begin CEXP1:. Because they are plain text they paste into anything, so a circuit can be handed to a class in a worksheet and opened by everyone in a couple of seconds.
