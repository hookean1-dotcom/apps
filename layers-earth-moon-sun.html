Key ideas

Earth's layers (1.3)

From the outside in: crust (thin solid shell), mantle (thick solid rock that flows slowly), outer core (liquid iron & nickel — makes the magnetic field), inner core (solid iron & nickel, kept solid by pressure).

The Moon compared (9.1)

The Moon has the same kinds of layers (crust, mantle, core with a solid inner and fluid outer part), but its core is much smaller (~20% of its radius vs Earth's ~55%) and its crust is proportionally thicker. Its tiny core means almost no magnetic field.

The Sun's interior (10.2–10.4)

Core (~15 million °C): fusion makes energy. Radiative zone: energy carried out by photons. Convective zone: energy carried by convection. Photosphere: the visible surface (~5,500 °C) where light escapes.

The Sun's atmosphere (10.5)

Above the surface: the chromosphere (thin, reddish) and the corona (faint, extremely low density, yet over a million °C — much hotter than the surface). Both are seen during a total solar eclipse.

How energy moves (10.3)

Made by fusion in the core → carried out by radiation → then by convection → finally radiated into space from the photosphere as sunlight.
