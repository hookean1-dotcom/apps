Transverse and longitudinal waves

Both waves below share the same amplitude, frequency and wavelength, and both carry energy from left to right. Watch what the particles do.

Transverse wave

The oscillations are perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer.

Longitudinal wave

The oscillations are parallel to the direction of energy transfer, producing compressions and rarefactions.

At this wavelength the longitudinal amplitude is drawn smaller, so that particles do not overtake one another.

What to notice

  • Each particle oscillates about a fixed position. The wave transfers energy, not matter.
  • Wave speed = frequency × wavelength. With the speed fixed, doubling the frequency halves the wavelength.
  • Wavelength is one complete wave: crest to crest, or compression to compression.

Examples

Transverse: electromagnetic waves such as light, ripples on water, waves on a string, seismic S-waves.

Longitudinal: sound waves, ultrasound, seismic P-waves, a pulse pushed along a slinky.
