    What is a superconductor?

    A material whose resistivity, and so resistance, becomes zero when it is cooled below its critical temperature, Tc.

    Each superconducting material has its own value of Tc. Above Tc it behaves like an ordinary conductor.

    The R–T graph

    • Superconductor: R falls gradually as T falls, then drops suddenly to zero at Tc and stays at zero below it.
    • Ordinary metal (copper): R falls as T falls but never reaches zero, even close to 0 K.

    Exam tip: draw a vertical drop at Tc, then a line along the T axis. Label Tc on the axis.

    Why a metal's resistance rises with T

    At higher temperature the positive ions in the lattice vibrate with greater amplitude. Free electrons collide with them more often, so their mean drift velocity is lower for the same p.d. and the resistance is greater.

    I = nAvq  ·  R = ρl / A

    Low vs high temperature

    • Low-temperature superconductors have Tc below 77 K, mostly metals and alloys (Hg, Pb, Nb, NbTi). They need liquid helium at 4.2 K, which is expensive and scarce.
    • High-temperature superconductors have Tc above 77 K, the boiling point of nitrogen. They are ceramic copper oxides (e.g. YBCO) and can be cooled with cheap, plentiful liquid nitrogen.

    "High" is relative: the record Tc at normal pressure is still about −139 °C.

    Uses

    With R = 0 a current flows with no energy dissipated as heat, so very large currents can produce very strong magnetic fields.

    • MRI scanners: coils of niobium–titanium wire cooled by liquid helium.
    • Particle accelerators: the LHC's 1232 dipole magnets use NbTi at 1.9 K, carrying about 11 800 A.
    • Also: maglev trains and low-loss power cables.

    Worked example

    A magnet coil carries a current of 400 A. Compare the power dissipated in a copper coil of resistance 0.050 Ω with a superconducting coil.

    Copper: P = I²R = 400² × 0.050 = 8000 W
    Superconductor: P = 400² × 0 = 0 W

    The copper coil would need 8 kW just to stay switched on, and that energy heats the coil.
