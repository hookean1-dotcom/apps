Method you are following

A length of bare resistance wire is taped alongside a metre rule. A flying lead makes contact at length L from the fixed terminal. The pd across the wire and the current through it are measured, so the resistance of that length is R = V/I.

Since R = ρL/A, a graph of R against L has gradient ρ/A, and the resistivity follows from the cross-sectional area found with the micrometer.

Why the graph and not a single reading? The contacts at each end add a small unwanted resistance. That offset shifts the whole line up but leaves the gradient untouched, so taking the gradient removes a systematic error that a single measurement of R/L would carry straight into your answer.

Plotting R against L

Choose your own scales, plot your six points, then draw the line of best fit and the steepest or shallowest line that still passes through every error bar.

The error bars are small, and that is the point. Digital meters reading to three figures give an uncertainty in R of only a percent or so, which is why the worst acceptable line sits so close to the best one. Draw it as steep or as shallow as the bars genuinely allow — no further.
