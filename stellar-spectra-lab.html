Key ideas

Three things from a spectrum (13.4)

A stellar spectrum reveals: chemical composition (from which absorption lines appear), temperature (from the colour and which lines are strongest), and radial velocity (from the Doppler shift of the lines).

Colour and temperature (13.6)

Hot stars glow blue-white and their spectrum peaks at short wavelengths; cool stars glow red and peak at long wavelengths. This is Wien's law — the peak wavelength is inversely proportional to temperature.

Spectral types (13.5)

Stars are sorted O B A F G K M from hottest (blue, ~40,000 K) to coolest (red, ~2,500 K). Remembered as “Oh Be A Fine Girl/Guy, Kiss Me.” The Sun is a G-type star.

Why A stars have the strongest H lines

All stars are mostly hydrogen, yet hydrogen lines are strongest in A-type stars (~9,500 K). It is about temperature, not amount: only at that temperature are hydrogen atoms in the right state to absorb these visible wavelengths. Hotter stars ionise the hydrogen; cooler stars leave it un-excited.

Radial velocity (13.4c)

If a star moves towards us its lines shift to shorter (blue) wavelengths; moving away, they shift to longer (red) wavelengths. The size of the shift gives the speed: v = c × (Δλ/λ).
