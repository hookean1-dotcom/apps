What am I looking at?

Each ball meets one peg per row and hops left or right with equal chance. After n rows, the bin it lands in depends only on how many times it went right, so the counts follow the binomial distribution B(n, ½).

There are far more routes to the middle bins than to the edges, so the middle fills fastest. With plenty of balls the pile takes the shape of the normal (bell) curve, with a spread of ½√n bins. Try 20 balls, then 2000.

Tilting the board makes one direction more likely at every peg. The pile shifts to a mean of np and its spread becomes √(npq), where q = 1 − p.

This is an ideal board: the balls fly in true projectile arcs, but every peg is an exactly fair, independent trial. A real board comes close, though bouncing balls carry some momentum from one peg to the next.
