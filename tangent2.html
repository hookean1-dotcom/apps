Method

Drawing a tangent, step by step

On a straight-line graph the gradient is the same everywhere. On a curve it changes, so you find the gradient at one point by drawing a tangent there: a straight line that touches the curve at that point and has the same slope as the curve there.

  1. Mark the pointGo up from the required x-value (e.g. t = 4.0 s) to the curve and mark it clearly with a small cross. Call it P.
  2. Lay the ruler against PRotate a clear ruler about P until it touches the curve there without cutting through it. Near P, the curve should sit on the same side of the ruler on both sides of the point.
  3. Check itLook along the ruler from both ends. The gaps between the ruler and the curve should open up evenly on each side of P. For extra accuracy use the mirror method (below).
  4. Draw it longDraw a thin, sharp pencil line right across the grid. A longer line is easier to read accurately.
  5. Draw a large triangleUse a triangle whose sloping side is at least half the length of your line. Choose corners where the line crosses grid lines, so you can read them exactly.
  6. Calculate and interpretgradient = Δy ÷ Δx. Keep the sign (a falling line has a negative gradient), give the unit (y-unit ÷ x-unit) and say what it means.

The mirror method

Stand a flat mirror upright across the curve at P. Turn it until the curve and its reflection join up smoothly, with no sharp corner at P. The mirror is then along the normal (at 90° to the curve). Draw along the mirror, then draw the tangent at right angles to that line. Try it with the Mirror method button in Explore: a kink in the dashed purple reflection means your tangent is off.

Tangent or chord?

A chord between two points gives the average gradient over that interval. It only matches the tangent as the two points close in on P. Turn on Chord in Explore and shrink it to watch this happen.

Common mistakes
  • Drawing a line between two data points (a chord), not a tangent.
  • Using a small triangle, so reading errors become a large fraction of Δx and Δy.
  • Counting squares instead of reading values off the axis scales, especially when an axis doesn't start at zero.
  • Leaving out the minus sign or the unit.
