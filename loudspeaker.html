What's happening — step by step

  1. An alternating current flows through the coil, which is wound on a tube attached to the cone.
  2. The coil sits in the radial magnetic field between the N and S poles of a permanent magnet.
  3. A current-carrying wire in a magnetic field feels a force — the motor effect (F = BIL). Fleming's left-hand rule gives its direction.
  4. The a.c. keeps reversing direction, so the force keeps reversing: the coil and cone vibrate in and out at the supply frequency.
  5. The cone pushes and pulls the air, making compressions and rarefactions — a longitudinal sound wave. Air particles vibrate parallel to the direction the wave travels.
