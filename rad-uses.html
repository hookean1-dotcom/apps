To choose a source, ask two questions. Which radiation can do the job (pass through, or be partly absorbed)? Which half-life suits the job (steady for years, or gone in hours)?

Irradiation or contamination?

Irradiation

An object is exposed to radiation from a source outside it. It stops as soon as the source is removed or shielded. The object does not become radioactive.

Contamination

Radioactive material gets onto or into an object or person. It keeps emitting radiation until it is removed or has decayed, so it is harder to deal with.

Alpha emitters are most dangerous if swallowed or breathed in: inside the body every alpha particle is absorbed by nearby cells.

Dangers and reducing the risk

  • Ionising radiation can damage cells and tissue and cause mutations in DNA, which may lead to cancer.
  • Time: spend as little time as possible near a source.
  • Distance: handle sources with long tongs and never point them at anyone.
  • Shielding: store sources in lead-lined boxes; use lead aprons or screens.
  • Workers wear dosimeter badges to monitor the dose they receive.
  • Radioactive waste with a long half-life must be stored securely for a very long time.
