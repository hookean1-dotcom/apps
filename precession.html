Key ideas (3.6 & 3.7)

What is precession?

The Earth spins like a top, but slowly wobbles as well: its axis traces a cone, taking about 25,800 years for one full turn. The tilt stays ~23.5°, but the direction the axis points keeps changing.

What causes it?

The Earth bulges at the equator. The gravity of the Sun and Moon pulls on this bulge and slowly swings the spin axis around, just like gravity makes a spinning top wobble.

Changing pole star (3.6)

As the axis points to different places, the star nearest the celestial pole changes. Today it is Polaris; around 2800 BC it was Thuban; in about 13,700 AD it will be Vega. The whole cycle repeats every ~26,000 years.

Precession of the equinoxes

The point where the Sun crosses the equator in spring (the First Point of Aries) slowly slides backwards through the zodiac — about 1° every 72 years, a new constellation every ~2,150 years. Star coordinates (RA and Dec) therefore change slowly over time.

Archaeoastronomy (3.6)

Ancient peoples aligned monuments to the sky. A shaft in the Great Pyramid (~2500 BC) pointed at Thuban, the pole star of the time; Stonehenge was aligned to the solstice Sun. Because of precession, these alignments are slightly off today — evidence of how the sky has shifted.
