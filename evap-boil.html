Boiling vs Evaporation

Two ways a liquid turns into a gas — and why they are not the same thing.

Evaporation — particle model

Why does evaporation cool a liquid?

  1. Particles in a liquid move at a range of speeds — they have a range of kinetic energies.
  2. Only particles at the surface that have enough energy to overcome the attractive forces from the other particles can escape.
  3. So the particles that escape are the most energetic ones.
  4. The average kinetic energy of the particles left behind decreases, so the temperature of the liquid decreases.

This is why sweating cools you down, and why a wet swimmer feels cold in a breeze.

Why doesn't the temperature rise while it boils?

While water boils, the heater is still supplying energy, but the temperature stays at the boiling point. The energy is used to overcome the attractive forces between the particles so they can separate and become a gas. This increases the internal energy of the water (the potential energy of the particles), not the average kinetic energy — so the temperature does not change.

The energy needed per kilogram is the specific latent heat of vaporisation (about 2.26 MJ/kg for water).

In common

  • Both change a liquid into a gas (vapour).
  • In both, particles need enough energy to overcome the attractive forces between them.
  • Both are physical changes — reversible, and mass is conserved.
  • Both need energy: the specific latent heat of vaporisation.

Everyday evaporation

  • Puddles drying after rain
  • Washing drying on a line — faster on a windy day
  • Sweat cooling your skin
  • Feeling cold getting out of a swimming pool
  • Salt left behind when sea water evaporates

Everyday boiling

  • A kettle boiling water
  • Cooking pasta in a pan of boiling water
  • Pasta taking longer to cook up a mountain (lower boiling point)
  • A pressure cooker cooking food faster (higher boiling point)

Common misconceptions

“Any bubbles mean the water is boiling.”
Small bubbles that appear early are dissolved air coming out of the water as it warms. Boiling bubbles are bubbles of water vapour.
“The white cloud from a kettle is steam.”
Water vapour is invisible. The cloud is tiny liquid droplets formed when the vapour condenses in cooler air.
“Turning up the heat makes boiling water hotter.”
At a fixed pressure it stays at the boiling point — it just boils away faster.
“Evaporation only happens when it's hot.”
It happens at any temperature — washing dries on a cold day.
“Water always boils at 100 °C.”
Only at standard atmospheric pressure (about 101 kPa).

Q: Explain why a liquid cools when it evaporates. [3 marks]

  • The particles with the most (kinetic) energy escape from the surface ✔
  • so the average kinetic energy of the remaining particles decreases ✔
  • so the temperature of the liquid decreases ✔

Q: Water is boiling in a pan. Explain why its temperature stays the same even though it is still being heated. [2 marks]
