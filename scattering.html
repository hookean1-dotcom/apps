Distance of closest approach

Fire an α straight at the nucleus. It stops when all its kinetic energy has turned into electrical potential energy, and that stopping distance is an upper limit on the radius of the nucleus — the α got that close and was still turned back by the charge alone.

What the experiment showed

Plum pudding, put to the test

Thomson’s atom spread the positive charge over the whole atom, so the field an α met was feeble and it could barely be deflected. Rutherford’s own words: “It was as if you fired a fifteen-inch shell at a piece of tissue paper and it came back and hit you.”
